About

The cameras were already there.

Sightline started with a simple observation: nearly every parking lot already has a camera pointed at it. The missing piece was software that reads what the camera sees — so that is the piece being built, by one person, in Charlotte.

hq Charlotte, North Carolina
The goal

Make every parking space visible — without installing new hardware, and without sending footage to someone else's cloud.

one sentence, two constraints. everything on this site is measured against it.

The story

One person, one observation.

Sightline is built by Monish Munagala in Charlotte, North Carolina. It began with arithmetic. In-ground sensors cost money for every single space, and installing them means construction: closing rows, cutting pavement, running power.

Gate counters only count entries and exits. They can tell you how many cars came in, but they can never tell a driver where an open space actually is.

The camera on the pole already sees every space at once. Sightline reads that view and turns it into live occupancy, one reading per space — RTSP stream in, YOLOv8-OBB oriented detection, polygon match, vote smoothing, live console out, all on your own hardware.

Principles

How the work gets done.

/01

Footage stays in your building

Sightline runs on your own hardware. Video is processed where it is captured and never leaves your network — only occupancy states, small structured records, move to the console.

/02

Count cars, not people

No face recognition and no license-plate reading — there is no ANPR anywhere in the system. The console knows a space is taken, not who took it.

/03

Every reading can be explained

Each occupancy decision traces to a measurable overlap between a detected car and a space outline. If a space changes state, Sightline can show you exactly why.

/04

It has to run for months, not demos

Streams reconnect on their own, no camera gets starved of processing time, and space states do not flicker. That unglamorous work is most of the product.

/05

Your hardware, your data

No proprietary boxes and no per-space sensor bills. If a camera speaks RTSP it is compatible, and every state, dwell, and peak curve it produces is yours to keep and export.

/06

Say how it works

This site explains how detection works, where it struggles, and what each threshold means — so you can check the readings against what you see on the ground.

Status

Pre-launch, and honest about it.

Sightline is early. There are no customer logos on this site because there are no customers yet — no invented testimonials, no rounded-up metrics, no team page with stock faces. What exists is a working instrument: one real camera on one real 100-space lot, detection running end to end at 5 fps, and a console reading it live in under a second.

That is also why the proof on this site is footage, not claims. Watch the live lot, read how Sightline compares — including where hardware honestly beats it — and if the arithmetic holds for your lot, bring a feed.
