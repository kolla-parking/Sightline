Solutions · three verticals

Three operations. One blind spot.

A garage operator, a campus, and a city curb program all run on the same missing number: which spaces are taken, right now. Sightline answers it with the cameras each of them already owns.

Operators & garages Campuses & venues Municipal curb
01 · Parking operators & garages

You can count the gate. You can't see the deck.

The job to be done

Revenue systems tell you how many cars came in. They don't tell you where anyone is. Loop counters drift and give totals, not locations; the difference between nearly full and full is someone walking a level with a clicker. And the peak hours you price and staff against are remembered, not measured.

What per-space truth changes

Sightline turns the CCTV already mounted in your facility into a live, per-space map. The booth sees exactly which stalls are open on which row the moment they change — Lot A resolves all 100 of its spaces from a single camera, analyzed at 5 fps, with changes on screen in under a second. And because history is measured in time occupied rather than detections fired, the peak-hour picture behind your rates is the real one.

02 · Campuses & venues

Demand arrives all at once. Your lots don't.

The job to be done

Class change, shift change, doors at seven — everyone arrives in the same ten minutes. The close lot overflows while the far one sits half empty, drivers circle, and staff direct traffic by radio and habit. The question a campus answers hundreds of times a day — is there parking — is answered with a guess.

What per-space truth changes

The cameras on buildings and poles already overlook most of your lots. Sightline gives each watched lot a live count and a per-space map, and several cameras report into one console, so the whole property reads as a single picture. On an event night you watch lots fill in real time and redirect before the circling starts. Across a semester, the hourly curves show which lots actually fill, and when — so permits, signage, and shuttle routes can follow the data instead of the complaints.

03 · Municipal curb management

The most contested asphalt in the city is the least measured.

The job to be done

The curb is split among loading zones, accessible spaces, metered stays, and no-stopping — and what actually happens there is known from occasional clipboard studies and complaint calls. A study covers a few blocks for a few days, costs real money, and goes stale the day the policy it informed takes effect.

What per-space truth changes

A traffic or security camera that already sees a block face is a continuous curb study waiting to be read. Sightline maps each curb space as its own polygon — parallel, angled, or loading, it is geometry either way — and records every occupancy change with a timestamp. Occupancy by hour, by space, continuously, becomes the evidence base for the next policy decision. And it runs on hardware the city controls: the footage never leaves the network, because what leaves the machine is state, not video. The data policy is written to be handed to a review board.

The retained record per curb space
space

Which curb space — an ID tied to a polygon drawn to the pavement. Parallel, angled, or loading: geometry either way.

state

occupied or free. One bit. No plate, no person in the record.

since

The timestamp of the last change — so the curb reads as time occupied, by hour, by space, continuously.
