01 · Camera software

Parquery

The closest thing to Sightline on the market: Swiss software that reads parking occupancy from images taken by cameras you already have. It has run for over a decade in more than 30 countries, and it is genuinely good.

Where Parquery wins

  • A long, proven track record across many climates and countries.
  • Add-ons Sightline does not offer today, including plate reading and enforcement workflows.
  • An in-camera option that runs directly on certain Axis cameras.

Where Sightline is different

  • Continuous analysis at 5 frames per second. Parquery works on snapshots, most commonly one per minute, so a space can be wrong for up to a minute.
  • On your hardware first. Parquery's default is processing in their cloud; their on-site option is calibrated remotely by their team.
  • Changes push to your dashboard the moment they are confirmed, not on the next snapshot cycle.

02 · Overhead sensors

Cleverciti now part of ParkHelp

Purpose-built overhead sensors with onboard AI, mounted on poles and masts. One unit can watch up to 100 spaces across a 220 degree field of view. Acquired by ParkHelp in late 2025 and folded into a full guidance and signage platform.

Where Cleverciti wins

  • On-street and curbside detection, where fixed security cameras rarely have coverage.
  • Processing happens inside the sensor, so only metadata leaves the device.
  • A complete guidance ecosystem: LED signage, enforcement routing, city integrations.

Where Sightline is different

  • No hardware to buy. Cleverciti detection requires purchasing and mounting their sensors; their software does not run on your cameras.
  • No poles, masts, or power runs. If your lot already has a camera view, you are done.
  • A lightweight data product you own, not a platform purchase.

03 · In-ground sensors

Nwave

Sealed wireless pucks that sit in or on the pavement, one per space, reporting over a LoRaWAN radio network. The classic hardware answer to occupancy, engineered well: 20 millimeters thin, rated for 3 tons per wheel, batteries specified up to 10 years.

Where Nwave wins

  • Works where no camera can see: curbs, low decks, isolated corners.
  • Per-space certainty from a device physically in the space, unaffected by fog, glare, or occlusion.
  • No video at all, which some privacy postures prefer.

Where Sightline is different

  • Zero devices in the pavement. A 100-space lot needs 100 Nwave sensors plus gateways; Sightline needs one camera view and a computer.
  • No battery lifecycle. Sensor batteries eventually mean per-space field work across the whole lot.
  • Visual context comes free: the same feed that counts spaces shows you the lot.

04 · Garage guidance

Park Assist M5 TKH Security

Camera-based guidance for parking structures: dedicated camera units mounted over every drive aisle, each watching up to six bays, with a red or green LED above every space. A mature product with a long record in airports, hospitals, and casinos.

Where Park Assist wins

  • Engineered viewing angles for every single bay, guaranteed by the install.
  • The over-space LED is guidance drivers can see from the aisle, which a dashboard cannot replace.
  • Plate reading and find-your-car features built into the same hardware.

Where Sightline is different

  • Your existing cameras are the sensor. Park Assist duplicates the CCTV you already bought with proprietary units on every aisle.
  • No construction. Their install runs ceiling channel, CAT6, and power cabinets through the whole structure.
  • Works on surface lots too. Ceiling-mounted architecture only fits covered garages.

05 · Payments and operations

Metropolis

The biggest name in AI parking, and the most different from Sightline. Metropolis installs its own cameras at entries and exits, reads plates, bills drivers automatically, and typically operates the facility itself. After acquiring SP+ it became the largest parking network operator in the United States.

Where Metropolis wins

  • End-to-end revenue: gateless entry, automatic payment, no tickets, no attendants.
  • Enormous scale, capital, and a consumer network of millions of members.
  • A single partner that takes the whole parking operation off your plate.

Where Sightline is different

  • It is a tool you own, not an operator you hire. Your staff, your rates, your data.
  • Per-space occupancy, not entry and exit sessions. Metropolis's public materials describe payment tracking, not a live map of which spaces are open.
  • Runs on your cameras and your hardware. No proprietary equipment, no platform dependency.

The short version

Different problems, different tools.

No cameras at all?

Sensors are the honest answer. Nwave for open lots and curbs, Cleverciti for streetside, Park Assist if you are refitting a garage anyway.

Want out of parking entirely?

Metropolis will run the whole thing, payments included. That is a management decision, not a technology one.

Cameras already watching your lot?

Then the sensor you need is already installed. Sightline turns it into live per-space data, on your hardware, with nothing new to buy or bury.
