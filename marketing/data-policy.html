Data policy · plain language

Occupancy, not identity.

Every camera needs an answer to "what does it collect?" This page is Sightline's, written to be read: what the system sees, what it keeps, and what it cannot do.

What is retained

The entire record, per space.

Sightline watches a camera feed and keeps one thing: the state of each parking space over time.

Three fields. That is the whole record. There is no frame, no image crop, no plate, and no person in it — occupancy history is useful for planning and audits, and useless for tracking anyone.

The retained record per space
space

Which stall — an ID tied to a polygon drawn on the pavement, not to any vehicle.

state

occupied or free. One bit. Nothing else.

since

The timestamp of the last change, so history reads as time occupied, not raw detections.

video retained: none · plates read: none
The commitments

What Sightline does — and cannot do.

These are properties of how the system is built, not settings that could quietly change.

/01

It detects occupancy, not identity

The model answers one question per space: is it occupied or free? It matches vehicles to the stalls they sit in. It does not recognize vehicles, faces, or people.

/02

There is no plate reading, anywhere

Sightline has no ANPR. Not switched off — absent. There is no component in the system that can read a license plate, so no policy, request, or mistake can produce one.

/03

Processing runs on the operator's own hardware

The camera streams to a machine inside the operator's building, and detection runs there. Footage never leaves the building — what leaves the machine is state, not video.

/04

State is retained. Video is not.

What accumulates is per-space state over time — occupied or free, with timestamps. Whatever the operator's existing CCTV recorder does, Sightline adds no video retention of its own.

What this means

For the people on both sides of the camera.

If you park in a watched lot

To Sightline, you are a rectangle of occupied pavement. The system records that a space filled and later emptied — not who you are, what you drive, or where you went next. It cannot answer "where is this car?", because it never learns which car is which.

Nothing about you accumulates. The lot's history is a log of spaces changing state, and it reads the same whether you parked there once or every day.

If you operate the lot

You can answer for every camera you point at your property. When a privacy review, a procurement office, or a resident asks what the system collects, the answer is one sentence: per-space occupancy state, processed on our own hardware.

This page is written to be handed over. Attach it to the RFP response, forward it to the review board — it says exactly what the software does, because that is all the software does.
